# Curriculum Workflow Guide

This guide is for departments, faculty proposers, curriculum committees, and authorized submitters preparing curriculum changes for formal workflow.

---

## Quick Start

If you are preparing a proposal:

1. Identify the likely review tier based on the scope of the change. If unclear consult the department curriculum committee or Associate Dean.
2. Complete and document the conversations expected for that pathway.
3. Prepare proposal materials appropriate to the scope of change.
4. Work with the authorized departmental submitter to enter the proposal in Kuali.

---

## Review Pathways

| Pathway | Examples | Preparation Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical / Cosmetic** | Course title correction, catalog description cleanup, minor language edit | Confirm the change has no curricular consequence. |
| **Course-Level Change** | New course, course modification, prerequisite change, credit-hour change, deactivation | Establish academic rationale, course structure, and local feasibility. |
| **Program Requirement Change** | Major/minor/concentration requirement changes, substantive program modification, new concentration in an existing program | Consult early with the Associate Dean; address student progression, advising, resources, and affected units. |
| **New Program or Major Restructuring** | New degree, new major, new graduate program, closure, major restructuring | Engage the Associate Dean during concept development and seek Provost-level concept discussion before formal submission. |

---

## Common Formal Flow

Most proposals move through a version of this flow:

1. Authorized departmental submitter enters proposal.
2. Technical/workflow review.
3. College review.
4. Senate review (FSCC; SGPC for graduate/professional proposals).

What differs by pathway is the work that should happen before submission, the information the proposal should contain, and the depth of review expected at each stage.

---

## Pre-Submission Expectations

| Pathway | Expected Before Submission |
|---|---|
| **Technical / Cosmetic** | Departmental authorization, normally from the chair, curriculum committee chair, or designated submitter. |
| **Course-Level Change** | Department curriculum committee review or equivalent departmental endorsement. |
| **Program Requirement Change** | Department endorsement plus documented Associate Dean consultation. |
| **New Program or Major Restructuring** | Sustained Associate Dean engagement during concept development plus Provost-level concept discussion before formal submission. |

The submission by an authorized submitter indicates that the expected local preparation has occurred.

---

## New Program Concept Discussion

New programs and major restructurings create institutional commitments. These proposals should not first appear as complete Kuali submissions.

Before formal submission, departments should work with the Associate Dean to develop a concept discussion that addresses:

- Rationale and intended student population.
- Evidence of demand or need.
- Relationship to existing UNM offerings.
- Broad resource requirements.
- Accreditation, state authorization, or reporting implications.
- Fit with college and institutional priorities.

The Provost-level concept discussion is a strategic check. It is not the full curricular review. Possible outcomes include proceed, proceed with modifications, or continue development before formal submission.

## Submitter Authorization

Formal submission should be limited to designated departmental submitters, typically the chair of the departmental curriculum committee or another designated faculty curriculum lead.

- Faculty proposers may develop proposals without serving as the formal submitter.
- Submission indicates departmental curriculum committee endorsement or equivalent authorization.
- Departments should maintain a current authorized-submitter roster.
- Procedures for updating rosters should be straightforward.
